Two people, one city

BelemConverse is built by Marco Mollinetti and Artur Nakauth Freires.

Marco Mollinetti

AI Division Manager, Nohara Group (Japan)

Marco leads BelemConverse. He runs the AI division at the Nohara Group, a Japanese construction company in its fourth century, where a team of three to five — three of them PhDs — carries nine concurrent projects across Japan, Singapore and Indonesia. The work runs from a constraint-programming and vision engine for gypsum board nesting to an OCR pipeline that reads six required fields off handwritten delivery faxes, alongside the group’s AI governance frameworks and its reskilling programmes.

He arrived there through research and engineering rather than around them. A Ph.D. in Systems Engineering from the University of Tsukuba, on deterministic methods for non-deterministic optimisation. A postdoc at CENSIPAM on autonomous oil-spill and vessel detection. Production work at Plimes on cough and swallow recognition, and at MTI on container scheduling across Japan, Vietnam and Singapore and on rain forecasting for aviation maps. As a research fellow at Lab-TeC he won a $200,000 CNPq grant to study lightning detection in the Amazon, with UFPA and the University of Arizona.

Belém is not a case study for him. He took his bachelor’s degree at the University Centre of Pará, finished first in his class, and still gives guest lectures on applied AI and optimisation at CESUPA. The city this system indexes is one he knows.

Artur Nakauth Freires

Cybersecurity, Chattahoochee Technical College (USA)

Artur is studying cybersecurity at Chattahoochee Technical College in Georgia, where he holds a 4.0 GPA, serves as treasurer for the National Technical Honor Society and has spoken as a guest in two of the college’s CIST courses. He came to security by way of data analytics — the Google Data Analytics certificate, then volunteer analysis work for the GLOCAL Foundation of Canada — and to data by way of games.

Games took him to Vancouver: a diploma in game design from Vancouver Film School, then QA at The Coalition, the Xbox studio behind Gears of War, where he was point of contact for the Map Builder feature and ran FPS, CPU and GPU performance testing. At EA Burnaby, through Keywords Studios, he became an area lead, running a team of more than twenty testers across three production cycles and working with producers and developers on scoping, risk tracking and play sessions.

He started in Belém. He took his computer science degree at CESUPA and published his first AI work there in 2012: a multilayer perceptron for movie ranking at the SBPC annual meeting, and AmAIzed, a tool for teaching artificial intelligence in high schools, at CNMAC. In the same years he co-founded HertzOne, a Belém esports organisation that ran League of Legends and Hearthstone tournaments for four years. Most recently he managed contracts and budgets for a hotel in Castanhal, east of the city — the kind of local business BelemConverse exists to point visitors toward.
